# Witness Workbenches

The Workbenches establish the textual evidence used in a collation. They precede Correspondence: a reading must first be identified, checked and documented before it can be aligned with material in another witness.

## Preparing a witness

An electronic text provides a starting point for verification, not an automatic scholarly endorsement. Researchers check it against the relevant facsimile or edition and record corrections. The application retains the distinction between the electronic starting text, its effective corrected form and the bibliographic source used for verification.

Edition text and comparison form are separate. Witness-specific policies govern features such as punctuation and diacritics; retained source references and comparison settings explain how a displayed reading relates to its verification source.

## Recording local evidence

| Workbench material | Research recorded before alignment |
| --- | --- |
| Masoretic witnesses | Verification of M-L against its facsimile and relevant local M-A/M-C attestations against their sources. |
| Greek | Verification of G, local Greek and related versional attestations, scope, source references and review status. |
| Latin, Syriac and Aramaic | Verification of V, S and T, corrections, local manuscript variants, support and source-side scholarly records. |
| DSS, where attested | The selected fragment and location, preserved material and relevant evidence; fragmentary preservation remains distinct from omission. |

Local readings have a scope. A variant from an apparatus is not treated as the text of a continuous witness outside its attestation. The record also distinguishes an attested reading from a researcher reconstruction. A source-side proposal is not automatically a preferred Hebrew reading in the final Core assessment.

The [source overview](sources-and-rights.md) lists the project's verification editions and default apparatus references. A default helps identify a source, but a precise locator and researcher confirmation are still needed. The Greek workflow allows confirmation of the default apparatus or specification of another source. Bibliographic verification does not remove the rights or provenance of electronic starting material.

## Verification and handover

Readiness is tied to the current source state. The preparation layer checks the selected sources and, for included local variants, their verification status and relationship to the current base text. Source changes can invalidate earlier selections and require renewed review.

Correspondence then selects the relevant verified material and links source-token spans across witnesses. It establishes where readings are comparable. It does not replace source verification, nor does it assign the later primary, secondary, literary or uncertain assessment.

| Stage | Question addressed |
| --- | --- |
| Workbench | What reading is attested or proposed here, on what evidence, and has it been checked? |
| Correspondence | Which textual spans and local attestations belong to the same comparison unit? |
| Core assessment | Which readings agree, partly agree or differ, and how should their relationship be interpreted? |

Research snapshots retain fuller working records. Publication packages present selected Approved results, their quoted readings and sources; they do not publish every Workbench record or supporting image. The [workflow](workflow.md) describes the remaining editorial and release stages.
